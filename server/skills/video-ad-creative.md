# Video Ad Creative

Build ads around the audience's current awareness and one dominant desire. The generator should produce several distinct angles, not many cosmetic rewrites of the same idea.

## Start with strategy

Identify:

- Awareness: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or most-aware
- Objective: awareness, conversion, or retargeting
- Dominant desire: health, attraction, status, belonging, control, or comfort
- Market sophistication: basic claim, stronger claim, new mechanism, better mechanism, or identity-led
- Product mechanism, proof, offer, objections, and compliant claims

Match the message to the stage:

- Unaware: pattern interrupt, curiosity, story, education, or entertainment.
- Problem-aware: name and intensify the pain, then introduce a credible path forward.
- Solution-aware: demonstrate the mechanism and distinguish the product from alternatives.
- Product-aware: handle objections, add proof, and make the offer easy to act on.
- Most-aware: use a timely offer, reminder, urgency, or customer-specific reason to return.

Do not lead with a hard sale to a cold audience. Warm people with useful or emotionally relevant content first.

## Hook system

The first 1–2 seconds must create curiosity, emotion, or a useful promise. Generate hooks from the angle, not from a generic list. Useful families include:

- Curiosity: reveal a surprising discovery or missing piece.
- Contrarian: challenge a common belief without making unsupported claims.
- Value: promise a specific result, lesson, or shortcut.
- Emotional story: create empathy, tension, or transformation.
- Demographic: call out the exact viewer and context.
- Proof or comparison: show a test, result, before/after, or alternative.
- Engagement: invite a choice, comment, save, or follow when it supports the goal.

Adapt the language to the platform. Keep TikTok/Reels punchy and native; make Facebook feed ads clear and scannable; let YouTube build more context. Never copy hook examples word for word—replace the placeholders with a truthful, specific insight.

## Script frameworks

Choose the framework that makes the product easiest to understand:

- Unboxing: arrival → reveal → first reaction → benefit → CTA.
- Testimonial: before state → skepticism → result → unique reason → low-risk CTA.
- Day in my life: routine → natural product use → recurring benefit → CTA.
- Problem/solution: frustrating pattern → failed alternatives → demonstration → result.
- Before/after: visible baseline → simple steps → transformed state → honest reaction.
- GRWM: natural routine → product use → comparison → finished result.
- Comparison/review: test alternatives → pros/cons → use-case winner → recommendation.
- Street interview: multiple genuine reactions → strongest reaction last → CTA.
- Tutorial: three simple steps → common mistake → correct approach → result.
- Story time: context → discovery → turning point → specific result → emotional CTA.

Write spoken language that sounds like a person, not a brand. Use specific details, natural reactions, visible proof, and a CTA that matches the platform. A UGC script should include what is shown, not just what is said.

## Psychology checks

Before approval, verify:

1. The ad captures an existing desire rather than trying to manufacture one.
2. It uses one dominant desire and one clear promise.
3. It starts from a belief the audience can accept, then builds gradually.
4. It makes the outcome tangible instead of listing empty benefits.
5. It shows how the product works whenever the claim needs proof.
6. It handles the strongest objection by changing the comparison or reducing risk.
7. It distinguishes the product from alternatives without unsupported attacks.
8. It looks native to the platform instead of like an over-produced commercial.

## Testing output

For each brief, return 3–5 concepts with:

- Concept name, awareness stage, objective, desire, and framework
- Hook options and the selected opening visual
- Shot-by-shot storyboard with spoken copy, overlays, and proof
- Product/offer/CTA moment and compliance notes
- Platform, duration, aspect ratio, and visual style
- A test matrix changing one major variable at a time: hook, desire, proof, format, or CTA

Prefer a small, meaningful test set over dozens of near-identical variants. Track hook retention, watch-through, click-through, conversion rate, and qualitative comments when results become available.

## Safety and quality

Do not invent testimonials, measurements, customer counts, medical outcomes, or product capabilities. Mark unsupported claims for review. Do not reproduce a competitor's ad verbatim; use saved examples as references for structure, pacing, and creative patterns.
